three main characteristics of data streams, that is, size, speed and data distribution, most data mining models are
incompatible with data stream mining.
   Stream processing is a computing paradigm that reads data in a stream from a collection of software or hardware
sensors and evaluates continuous data streams, with feedback outputs.    In the recent decade, concept drift has been a commonly researched subject in the domain of streaming data, with
comprehensive surveys on concept drift detection methods. However, the issue is that the available literature is fairly frag-
mented, and comparable concepts are produced under various taxonomies. In addition to an analysis of existing state-of-
the-art approaches, this study aims to elicit discussion and assist readers in the identification of relevant algorithms suit-
able for their application scenarios. 3.3    |  Types of drifts


The distribution of data streams is dynamic as they are continuous in nature. The data trends may change with time,
and their distribution may differ. Such changes in the data distribution may be broadly categorized into two types on
the basis of change in decision boundary, that is, (a) real concept drift and (b) virtual concept Drift. The categorization
of drifts can also be done on the basis of the speed of concept change, that is, by dividing them into five major types,
namely (i) abrupt (Sudden), (ii) gradual, (iii) incremental, (iv) recurring and (v) blip. tion, adaptation and performance evaluation techniques. This article also discusses current and anticipated trends in
