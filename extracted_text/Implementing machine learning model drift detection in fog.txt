Abstract
Machine learning (ML) is an active area of research for solving classification and regression problems. ML models are
used in numerous fields ranging from medicine to engineering, social science, and business. When deployed after training,
these models generate predictions to support decision-making. However, trained models may perform less effectively on
new, unseen data than during training; their performance may deteriorate over time. Such deterioration can result from
concept drift, where the underlying data distribution changes over time and begins to exhibit patterns that differ from
those in the training data. This is especially critical in distributed environments such as fog computing, where drift may
occur independently across multiple fog devices. In this paper, we focus on drift detection for real-time healthcare envi­
ronments, where a deployed ML model (typically a classifier) processes unlabeled data. Our Real-time Drift Detection
Method (RTDDM) detects drift by integrating supervised and unsupervised learning: we simultaneously train a classifier
and fit a clustering model on the same dataset, thereby linking classifier accuracy to cluster purity. During deployment,
we compare the classifier’s predicted label for each unlabeled instance with the class label assigned to its cluster, yielding
an approximate error signal that can be used for drift detection. The experiments show that our proposed method reduces
the need for human intervention.
